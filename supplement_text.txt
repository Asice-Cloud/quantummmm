INFLUENCE OF THE DISTANCE BETWEEN TWO PARALLEL SEMICONDUCTOR-SUPERCONDUCTING NANOWIRE

The modified “tetron” qubit is connected through a trivial superconductor. In this case, the length of the trivial superconductor
is hard to be short. Here we study the effect induced by the length of the trivial superconductor. Fig. S2 shows the evolution of
ψ1+ (t) with different lengths of the trivial superconductor Nc as Nc = 6 in Fig. S2(a), and Nc = 9 in Fig. S2(b). We can see that
the non-Abelian braiding properties are well preserved in both these two cases. The corresponding evolution of the eigenenergy
spectrum during the braiding process is shown in Fig. S2(c) and Fig. S2(d), respectively. We can see that the bulk gap decreases
with the increase of Nc . However, the gap remains open even when Nc is in the order of the superconducting coherence length.
This means that the non-Abelian braiding properties can be well preserved when the length of the trivial superconductor Nc is
smaller than the superconducting coherence length.

III.

SUPERCONDUCTING PHASE KNOB FOR THE TETRON STRUCTURE

Because the tetron qubit consists of two parallel-aligned nanowires, a domain wall structure will be formed at the position that
W 2 and W 4 intersect the trivial superconductor when t = 2T . Figure S3(a) shows that the energy of the bound states decrease
to zero at t = 2T , indicating that an additional pair of zero modes will emerge from the bulk at the domain wall. These two
extra modes will cause information leakage to the bulk states and therefore ruin the braiding [see Fig. S1(b)]. Fortunately, a
superconducting phase knob is provided to preserve the robustness of the non-Abelian braiding. By tuning the superconducting
phase difference φ0 to a non-zero value, the domain wall could be smoothed out. Hence the energy gap remains open throughout
the braiding process [Fig. 1(e) in the main text] and the non-Abelian braiding properties remain [Fig. 1(f) in the main text].
Superconducting phase can smooth out the domain wall structure and enlarge the bulk gap. Here we study the variance of the
bulk gap as a function of the superconducting phase. Since the bulk gap is minimized when the nanowires are fully connected,
we consider the variance of the bulk gap versus the superconducting phase in the different connection conditions of the voltage
gates. Fig. S4 shows the lowest four eigenenergies versus the superconducting phase difference φ0 . Here, G1, G3, and G4
are connected when φ0 ∈ (0, 2π); G1, and G4 are connected when φ0 ∈ (2π, 4π); G1, G2, and G4 are connected when
φ0 ∈ (4π, 6π); G2 and G4 are connected when φ0 ∈ (6π, 8π); G2, G3, and G4 are connected when φ0 ∈ (8π, 10π); G3 and
G4 are connected when φ0 ∈ (10π, 12π). We can see that the bulk gap is optimized if φ0 = 0 in the time period t ∈ (0, T ) and
t ∈ (2T, 3T ), while φ0 = π in the time period t ∈ (T, 2T ).

shares the similar behavior as that exhibited in the main text.
